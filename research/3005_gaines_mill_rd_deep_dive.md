# Research Report: 3005 Gaines Mill Rd, Gainesville, GA

## Property Overview
- **Address:** 3005 Gaines Mill Rd, Gainesville, GA 30507
- **County:** Hall
- **Price:** $120,000
- **Acreage:** 9.40 Acres
- **Price/Acre:** ~$12,766 (Well below budget mandate of <$50k/acre)
- **Status:** Active

## Zoning & Subdivision Feasibility
- **Current Zoning:** **R-I (Residential-I)**
- **Subdivision Requirements:** As of 2022, Hall County requires a **Conditional Use Permit (CUP)** approved by the Board of Commissioners for any subdivision within the R-I district.
- **Min Lot Size (Public Sewer + Water):** **35,000 sq. ft. (~0.80 acres)**
- **Feasibility:** 
    - **4-Lot Subdivision:** **Highly Feasible.** The 9.4-acre tract provides more than double the required land for 4 lots, even after accounting for environmental constraints.
    - **Subdivision Type:** Will likely require a "Minor Subdivision" process but must first clear the R-I Conditional Use Permit requirement.
    - **Frontage:** Sufficient road frontage exists on Gaines Mill Rd, but the "Steep Slope" terrain may limit the number of direct-access driveways. A shared internal drive or a hammerhead turnaround may be required.

## Road & Infrastructure Options
- **Sewer Availability:** **High Potential.** The property is adjacent to the **Avery Ridge** subdivision (Centex/Pulte), which is served by public sewer. This is a significant advantage over other septic-reliant leads.
- **Strategy:** Priority is to secure a **Sewer Availability Letter** from Hall County Public Works. If sewer connection is feasible (topography allowing for gravity flow), the lot yield could potentially increase or septic risks would be eliminated.
- **Road:** Gaines Mill Rd is a paved county road. No new public road construction is anticipated for a 4-lot split, but a private shared drive may be necessary to navigate the steep grade.

## Environmental & Infrastructure
- **Water/Sewer:** Public water and sewer likely available.
- **Topography:** **"Steep Slope"** designation. The land drops significantly from the road toward the creek at the rear. 
    - **Implication:** Homes will likely require **Daylight Basements**. Construction costs may be higher due to grading and foundation requirements.
- **Soil Analysis (Preliminary):**
    - **Pacolet Series (PaE/PaF):** Dominant on steep slopes. Well-drained but prone to erosion. Severe limitations for standard septic but manageable for foundations with proper engineering.
    - **Madison/Cecil Series:** Likely present on the flatter portions near the road.
- **Water Features:** **Creek/Stream** present at the rear of the property.
- **Flood Zone:** **Partial.** A portion of the acreage near the creek is in **Zone A** (High Risk). However, the majority of the 9.4 acres is in **Zone X** (Minimal Risk) and remains buildable.
- **Buffers:** 50ft to 75ft state/county water buffers will apply around the creek, reducing the "net" usable acreage, though the 9.4-acre total provides ample buffer room.

## Location & Commute
- **Mall of Georgia:** ~25-30 minutes (Passes 40m mandate).
- **MARTA Railyard (Downtown ATL):** ~55-65 minutes (Passes 1h15m mandate).

## Critical Risks
1. **Conditional Use Permit (CUP):** Subdivision is not "by right" in R-I; requires Board of Commissioners approval.
2. **Topography:** The "Steep Slope" may make building 4 distinct pads more expensive or limit the placement of homes to a narrow strip near the road.
3. **Sewer Connection Cost:** While sewer is nearby, the cost to extend the line and potentially install a lift station (if gravity flow isn't possible due to the slope) must be evaluated.

## Recommendation
**PROCEED / HIGH PRIORITY.** 
The price-per-acre is exceptional, and the **potential for public sewer** makes this a much safer long-term investment than septic-only lots. The large acreage (9.4 acres) easily absorbs the losses from the creek/flood zone and steep slopes while still allowing for 4+ oversized lots.

**Next Steps:**
1. Request **Sewer Availability Letter** from Hall County Public Works.
2. Conduct a **Topographic Survey** to identify buildable "shelf" areas outside of the steep slope and flood buffers.
3. Consult with a civil engineer regarding the **Conditional Use Permit** process for R-I subdivisions in Hall County.
