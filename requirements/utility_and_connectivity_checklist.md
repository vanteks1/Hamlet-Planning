# Utility & Connectivity Checklist

In rural Jackson, Barrow, and Hall counties, utility availability varies block by block. This checklist establishes a standard verification protocol to run on every parcel before making an offer. One side of the road can have 1Gbps fiber while the other is on satellite — this document ensures we never get surprised.

---

## Verification Protocol Overview

Run these checks in order. Steps 1–4 can be done in under 30 minutes using free online tools. Steps 5–7 require a phone call.

---

## 1. Public Water vs. Well

**Why it matters:** Public water at the road eliminates well drilling costs ($6,000–$15,000/lot) and the risk of poor water yield. It also removes the need to reserve land area for well heads and setbacks.

**How to verify:**
- Call the county Water & Sewer Authority and give them the parcel address/tax ID.
- Ask: *"Is there an active water main on the road frontage at this address, and what is the connection fee?"*
- Check if the main is on the **same side of the road** as the parcel — crossing a state highway to tap a main on the opposite side requires a DOT bore permit and adds $5,000–$15,000.

**What to record:**
- [ ] Public water available: Yes / No
- [ ] Distance from property line to nearest main: _____ ft.
- [ ] Connection/tap fee: $_____
- [ ] Monthly service base rate: $_____
- [ ] Notes: _________________________________________________

**County contacts:**
- Barrow: Barrow County Water: (770) 307-3021
- Jackson: Jackson County Water & Sewerage Authority: (706) 367-6011
- Hall: Hall County Water Resources: (770) 531-6950
- Gwinnett: Gwinnett County Dept. of Water Resources: (678) 376-6900

## 2. Public Sewer vs. Septic

**Why it matters:** If public sewer is available, septic systems (and the "usable soil" issue) are eliminated entirely. If not, septic is required for every dwelling — and ADUs/MIL suites increase the required drainfield area. See the [Septic & Usable Soil Reference](septic_and_usable_soil.md) for sizing math.

**How to verify:**
- Ask the same Water & Sewer contact as above: *"Is there an active sewer line within 500 feet of this parcel?"*
- In rural areas, sewer availability is almost always No — but worth confirming, especially on parcels near town centers.

**What to record:**
- [ ] Public sewer available: Yes / No
- [ ] Distance to nearest sewer main: _____ ft.
- [ ] Connection/tap fee if available: $_____
- [ ] Notes: _________________________________________________

## 3. Electrical Service

**Why it matters:** All parcels in the target counties should have power available, but the service capacity and provider affects EV charging, HVAC, and workshop loads for 4 households on a shared infrastructure.

**How to verify:**
- Identify the electric provider from the county tax map or ask the listing agent.
- Confirm whether the parcel is in a **cooperative (EMC)** or **investor-owned utility (IOU)** service territory — EMCs sometimes have longer lead times for new service installation.
- Ask the utility: *"Is there a transformer within 200 feet of the parcel? What is the new service connection fee for a 200A residential service?"*

**Georgia EMCs relevant to target counties:**
- Barrow/Jackson: **Walton EMC** (waltonemco.com) or **Georgia Power** depending on parcel
- Hall: **Jackson EMC** (jacksonemc.com)
- Forsyth/Gwinnett: **Georgia Power**

**What to record:**
- [ ] Utility provider: _______________________________
- [ ] Service type: EMC / IOU
- [ ] Transformer within 200 ft: Yes / No / Unknown
- [ ] New service connection fee: $_____
- [ ] Notes: _________________________________________________

## 4. Natural Gas vs. Propane

**Why it matters:** Natural gas is dramatically cheaper for heating and cooking than propane. In rural areas, propane is often the only option, which changes HVAC design and monthly operating costs.

**How to verify:**
- Check Atlanta Gas Light's online service area map (aglc.com) — search by parcel address.
- If not on the map, assume propane only.
- Ask the county planning office: *"Are there any planned gas line extensions in [road name / area]?"*

**What to record:**
- [ ] Natural gas available at road: Yes / No
- [ ] If No, nearest gas main: _____ miles
- [ ] Gas main extension cost (if any): $_____
- [ ] Notes: _________________________________________________

## 5. High-Speed Internet / Fiber Availability

**Why it matters:** This is a non-negotiable quality-of-life requirement for four remote-working families. In rural Georgia, one side of the road can have 1Gbps fiber while the other side is on satellite or fixed wireless with 25Mbps and high latency.

**Verification tools (run all three — they disagree frequently):**

| Tool | URL | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| FCC Broadband Map | broadbandmap.fcc.gov | Official baseline |
| Georgia Broadband Availability Map | broadband.georgia.gov | State-level fiber grants |
| ISP direct check | See list below | Most accurate |

**ISPs serving rural NE Georgia:**
- **Fiber options:** Windstream/Kinetic, Sawnee EMC Fiber, North Georgia EMC Fiber, Jackson EMC Fiber, Habersham EMC Fiber
- **Cable/DSL:** Xfinity (Gwinnett/Forsyth only), AT&T
- **Fixed wireless:** Farmwave, local WISPs
- **Satellite (last resort):** Starlink (~150–200 Mbps, ~$120/mo, high latency)

**How to verify (most reliable method):**
1. Go to the ISP's website and enter the **parcel address** (not nearby addresses — check the actual parcel).
2. Call the ISP directly if the online tool shows "Not available" — online tools are often outdated.
3. Ask neighbors: knock or check Facebook groups for the area. Local knowledge is more reliable than any map.

**What to record:**
- [ ] Provider 1: _____________ | Speed: _______ up / _______ down | Type: Fiber / Cable / DSL / Fixed Wireless
- [ ] Provider 2: _____________ | Speed: _______ up / _______ down | Type: Fiber / Cable / DSL / Fixed Wireless
- [ ] Fiber confirmed at address: Yes / No / Planned (date: ______)
- [ ] Notes: _________________________________________________

## 6. Cell Coverage

**Why it matters:** During construction and as a backup to fixed internet. 4 families with different carriers need sufficient signal.

**How to verify:**
- Check coverage maps for AT&T, Verizon, and T-Mobile at the parcel's GPS coordinates.
- T-Mobile has the best rural coverage in NE Georgia generally, but Verizon is better in some specific rural pockets.

**What to record:**
- [ ] AT&T: Strong / Moderate / Weak / No signal
- [ ] Verizon: Strong / Moderate / Weak / No signal
- [ ] T-Mobile: Strong / Moderate / Weak / No signal

---

## 7. Trash & Mail Service

**Why it matters:** Rural parcels sometimes lack county trash collection, requiring haul-away service. Mail delivery gaps affect package delivery logistics.

**How to verify:**
- Call county solid waste: *"Does [road name] have residential trash pickup?"*
- Check USPS address lookup tool for mail delivery status.

**What to record:**
- [ ] County trash pickup: Yes / No (If No, private haul service: ~$40–$60/month)
- [ ] USPS mail delivery: Yes / No (P.O. Box required)

## Strategy Notes

1. **Fiber is the tiebreaker.** All else being equal, prioritize parcels with confirmed fiber over those on fixed wireless or satellite. The productivity and quality-of-life difference for 4 remote-working families is significant.
2. **Jackson EMC Fiber** is actively expanding in Jackson County and parts of Hall County. Check their expansion map — parcels in planned zones may get fiber within 12–18 months of purchase.
3. **Public water is a $30k–$60k swing.** A parcel with public water at the road is worth paying a premium for — it eliminates well drilling costs for all 4 lots and removes the water-yield risk.
4. **Never trust one ISP's availability map.** Always cross-reference with the FCC broadband map AND a direct call to the ISP. The gap between what a map shows and what's actually installed at a specific address is surprisingly large in rural areas.
