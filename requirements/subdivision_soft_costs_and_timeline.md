# Subdivision "Soft Cost" & Timeline Estimator

A $200k lot can easily become a $250k–$275k lot once permits, studies, and infrastructure are accounted for. This checklist maps every cost from "Raw Land Contract" to "Ready to Pull Building Permits" for a 4-lot hamlet subdivision in the Georgia NE / Atlanta metro fringe region.

---

## Phase 1: Due Diligence (Before Closing)

*Goal: Confirm the land is actually buildable for our use case. These costs are spent pre-purchase and are at-risk if we walk away.*

| Item | Who Does It | Est. Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 1 Soil Feasibility** | Licensed soil scientist | $300–$600 | 2–5 days |
| **Boundary Survey (existing)** | Licensed surveyor (confirm existing markers) | $500–$1,500 | 1–2 weeks |
| **Title Search** | Real estate attorney | $300–$500 | 3–5 days |
| **Zoning Verification Letter** | County planning office (self-request) | $0–$100 | 1–3 days |
| **Environmental Desktop Review** | Internal (FEMA flood map + wetlands layer) | $0 | Same day |
| **Phase I Environmental Site Assessment** | Environmental firm (if prior commercial use) | $1,500–$3,500 | 2–3 weeks |

**Phase 1 Subtotal: ~$2,600–$6,200**

---

## Phase 2: Subdivision Design & Approval

*Goal: Get the subdivision plat approved and recorded. This is where the process gets expensive and slow.*

| Item | Who Does It | Est. Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Level 2/3 Soil Study** | Soil scientist + field work | $1,500–$3,500 | 1–3 weeks |
| **Boundary & Topographic Survey** | Licensed surveyor | $3,000–$6,000 | 2–4 weeks |
| **Subdivision Plat Design** | Civil engineer or surveyor | $2,500–$5,000 | 3–6 weeks |
| **Percolation Tests (per lot)** | County Environmental Health | $150–$400/lot | 1–4 weeks (scheduling lag) |
| **Preliminary Plat Application Fee** | County planning | $500–$1,500 | Paid at submission |
| **Final Plat Review Fee** | County planning | $500–$1,500 | Paid at final |
| **Plat Recording Fee** | Clerk of Superior Court | $100–$300 | Same day (at recording) |
| **Variance Application (if needed)** | County BOA | $300–$800 | 4–8 weeks (hearing cycle) |

**Phase 2 Subtotal: ~$8,550–$24,000**
*(High end assumes variance is needed and engineer hours are significant.)*

---

## Phase 3: Infrastructure Permitting

*Goal: Get approval to physically disturb the land and build the road/driveway and utility connections.*

| Item | Who Does It | Est. Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LDP (Land Disturbance Permit)** | County engineering | $500–$2,500 | 4–12 weeks |
| **Erosion & Sediment Control Plan** | Civil engineer (required for LDP) | $1,500–$3,500 | 3–5 weeks |
| **Stormwater Management Plan** | Civil engineer (if disturbing > 1 acre) | $2,000–$5,000 | Included in LDP package |
| **NPDES Permit (if > 1 acre disturbed)** | GA EPD (self-file with LDP) | $0–$150 | Filed with LDP |
| **DOT Driveway / Curb Cut Permit** | GA DOT or county roads dept. | $100–$500 | 2–6 weeks |
| **Private Road / Shared Driveway Plan** | Civil engineer | $1,500–$4,000 | 3–5 weeks (included in LDP package often) |

**Phase 3 Subtotal: ~$5,600–$15,650**

---

## Phase 4: Infrastructure Construction

*Goal: Build the road, rough-grade lots, and stub utilities to each lot line.*

| Item | Who Does It | Est. Cost | Timeline |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Road/Driveway Grading & Base** | Grading contractor | $15,000–$40,000 | 2–4 weeks |
| **Asphalt Paving (if required)** | Paving contractor | $10,000–$30,000 | 1–2 weeks (after base) |
| **Culverts & Drainage** | Grading contractor | $2,000–$8,000 | With grading |
| **Well Drilling (per lot, if no public water)** | Well driller | $6,000–$15,000/lot | 1–3 days/lot |
| **Septic System Installation (per lot)** | Licensed septic contractor | $8,000–$20,000/lot | 3–5 days/lot |
| **Utility Trench (electric, telecom rough-in)** | Utility/electrical contractor | $2,000–$6,000/lot | 1–2 weeks |

**Phase 4 Subtotal (4-lot, no public water): ~$125,000–$340,000**
*(This is infrastructure only — does not include house construction.)*

---

## Grand Total Summary

| Phase | Low Estimate | High Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Due Diligence | $2,600 | $6,200 |
| Subdivision Design & Approval | $8,550 | $24,000 |
| Infrastructure Permitting | $5,600 | $15,650 |
| Infrastructure Construction | $125,000 | $340,000 |
| **Total Soft Cost + Infrastructure** | **~$142,000** | **~$386,000** |
| **Per Lot (4 lots)** | **~$35,500** | **~$96,500** |

> **Practical rule:** Budget $50,000–$75,000 per lot in pre-construction costs on top of the land purchase price when evaluating raw parcels for a 4-lot hamlet.

---

## Timeline: Raw Land to Ready-to-Build

```
Month 1–2:   Due diligence, soil study, boundary survey
Month 2–4:   Subdivision plat design + county submission
Month 4–6:   County review cycle (preliminary plat approval)
Month 6–8:   LDP application + engineering plan set
Month 8–10:  LDP approval (can overlap with final plat)
Month 10–12: Final plat recording
Month 12–16: Infrastructure construction (road, wells, septic)
Month 16+:   Building permits pulled per lot
```

**Realistic total timeline: 14–20 months from contract to pulling the first building permit.**

## Red Flag Cost Escalators

| Trigger | Added Cost | Added Time |
|---|---|---|
| Variance required (frontage/setbacks) | +$1,000–$3,000 | +2–4 months |
| Wetlands delineation required | +$2,000–$5,000 | +4–8 weeks |
| Paved road required (vs. gravel) | +$15,000–$40,000 | +2–3 weeks |
| Public water extension needed | +$10,000–$50,000+ | +3–6 months |
| Floodplain mitigation required | +$5,000–$25,000+ | +2–6 months |
| Phase II Environmental (contamination found) | +$5,000–$50,000+ | +2–6 months |

## Pre-Offer Checklist

- [ ] Run a flood zone check (FEMA FIRM map) on the parcel — any AE zone is a major cost risk.
- [ ] Confirm whether public water is available at the road — private wells for 4 lots add ~$30,000–$60,000.
- [ ] Ask the county if a LDP is required for your specific lot count and disturbed acreage.
- [ ] Get a preliminary estimate from a local civil engineer before making an offer (many do a 30-minute free consult).
- [ ] Request a copy of any prior surveys, perc tests, or permits from the seller — reduces Phase 1 and 2 costs significantly.
