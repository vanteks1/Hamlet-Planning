# Septic & "Usable Soil" Reference

Septic feasibility is the #1 reason rural subdivisions fail in Georgia. County Health Departments — not the planning office — have final say. A lot can be perfectly zoned and still be unbuildable if the soil won't perc.

---

## How Georgia Septic Sizing Works

Georgia EPD uses a "usable soil" model. Each county (via its Environmental Health office) designates how much qualifying soil area is required **per bedroom**, and the soil must achieve a minimum percolation rate ("Code" classification).

**Key terms:**
- **Code A Soil:** Best perc rate. Fastest absorption. Fewest linear feet of drainfield required.
- **Code B / C Soil:** Slower absorption. Requires more drainfield area per bedroom.
- **Limiting Zone:** The depth at which rock, clay, or seasonal high water table begins. Must be ≥ 24–36 inches from the surface (varies by county) for a standard system.
- **Level 1 Soil Study:** Visual inspection + basic perc test. Used for initial feasibility (~$300–$600).
- **Level 2/3 Soil Study:** Deep borings + detailed mapping. Required before subdivision plat approval (~$1,500–$3,500).

## County-by-County "Usable Soil" Requirements

### 1. Barrow County
- **Minimum usable soil per lot:** **25,500 sq. ft.** of Code A (or equivalent)
- **Per bedroom:** ~3,000–4,500 sq. ft. of drainfield area (Code A)
- **Setbacks:** 10 ft from property line; 50 ft from well; 10 ft from structures
- **MIL Suite impact:** A 2-bed MIL suite on the same septic system adds ~6,000–9,000 sq. ft. to your required usable soil. A shared system for a main house + MIL is typically allowed but must be designed together.
- **County contact:** Barrow County Environmental Health: (770) 307-3034

### 2. Jackson County
- **Minimum usable soil per lot:** ~**20,000–25,000 sq. ft.** (varies by soil code; confirm with EH)
- **Per bedroom:** ~2,500–4,000 sq. ft. drainfield (Code A/B)
- **Setbacks:** 10 ft from property line; 50 ft from well
- **MIL Suite impact:** Detached ADUs on a shared septic system are allowed with a combined design. Separate systems for the ADU require their own dedicated usable soil area — effectively doubling the land requirement.
- **County contact:** Jackson County Environmental Health: (706) 367-5340

### 3. Hall County
- **Minimum usable soil per lot:** ~**22,500 sq. ft.** of qualifying soil
- **Per bedroom:** ~3,000 sq. ft. (Code A)
- **Setbacks:** Standard Georgia setbacks apply; 75 ft from streams
- **MIL Suite impact:** Hall County allows shared septic for main + ADU if designed as one system upfront. Retrofitting a shared system later is costly and may require re-evaluation.
- **County contact:** Hall County Environmental Health: (770) 531-3100

### 4. Gwinnett County
- **Minimum usable soil per lot:** Public sewer is required in most zones. Septic is only allowed in very rural/AG parcels.
- **Per bedroom:** If septic is permitted: ~3,500–5,000 sq. ft. (soil quality is generally poorer in Gwinnett)
- **MIL Suite impact:** Gwinnett strongly prefers sewer for any secondary unit. If septic, each dwelling unit may require its own system.
- **Note:** Gwinnett is largely sewered — rural parcels on septic are rare and scrutinized heavily.

### 5. Forsyth County
- **Minimum usable soil per lot:** ~**25,000 sq. ft.** (Code A equivalent)
- **Per bedroom:** ~3,500–4,500 sq. ft.
- **Setbacks:** 10 ft from property line; 50 ft from well; 25 ft from streams
- **MIL Suite impact:** Forsyth EH is stricter — separate ADU septic systems are typically required unless the ADU is very small (< 2 bed). Combined systems require documented usable soil for total bedroom count.

## The "4-Family + MIL Suite" Math

Assume: 4 main houses (3 bed each) + 2 MIL suites (2 bed each) = **16 total bedrooms**

| Scenario | Bedrooms | Approx. Usable Soil Needed (Code A) |
|---|---|---|
| 4 houses, no ADU | 12 | ~48,000 sq. ft. (~1.1 acres of usable soil) |
| 4 houses + 2 MIL suites | 16 | ~64,000 sq. ft. (~1.5 acres of usable soil) |
| 4 houses + 4 MIL suites | 20 | ~80,000 sq. ft. (~1.8 acres of usable soil) |

> **Rule of thumb:** On a 10-acre parcel, you realistically have ~6–7 acres of "usable" land after slopes, setbacks, and buffers. This is usually sufficient — but a Level 2 soil study is non-negotiable before committing to purchase.

## Pre-Offer Checklist

- [ ] Request a **Level 1 soil feasibility opinion** from a licensed soil scientist before making an offer (informal, ~$300–$500, done in 1–2 days).
- [ ] Confirm the lot's limiting zone depth with Barrow/Jackson EH directly.
- [ ] Ask the listing agent if a **prior perc test** was ever done — results are often on file with the county.
- [ ] Verify the **number of bedrooms** each family plans to build. More bedrooms = larger drainfield.
- [ ] Flag any property with slopes > 25% or visible creek drainages — these severely limit drainfield placement.

---

## Strategy Notes

1. **Soil beats zoning.** A lot that passes zoning review can still be killed at the EH office. Always run soil feasibility in parallel with zoning research.
2. **Individual systems vs. community system.** For 4 separate lots, 4 individual septic systems are the norm. A community drainfield (shared system for multiple homes) exists but requires a licensed operator and EPD community system permit — far more complex and expensive.
3. **Reserve area.** Georgia requires a designated repair/replacement drainfield area. That doubles your effective usable soil requirement. A 12-bedroom development needs ~96,000–128,000 sq. ft. of identified soil area (primary + reserve).
